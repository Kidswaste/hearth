# fps107 (round 107): nothing draws or wakes more than it needs to on a 240 Hz screen, after twenty rounds of new code

Round 84 (`gpuload84.md`) capped and paused what drew at the owner's 240 Hz screen rate (Windows' audio crackled in
Spotify and the DAW from the graphics card's load). Since then a lot of new code runs per frame or in idle moments:
the Lab's shader compiles in the gaps after frames (labcompile90, modelcompile101), the picture slice queues (labcompile90,
modeltex100), the `decOff` picture worker, the `__labHitch` / `__labPicsSettle` probes, chatwin's pieces per frame
(cw103), chatsafe's reader in parts (checksgc104), Lazy's idle loading (labload94), chatwin's idle record reads, the sync
panel, the kept-aside view. This round measured every state the owner is in, again, the way gpuload84 did.

## How I measured: `dev/checks/fps107.js`
- A **240 Hz screen is faked** in Hearth's window **and in the Lab preview's own page** (new: gpuload84 could only fake
  it in the window and the Lab's tool pages; the preview page captures its frame function when it loads). The counters
  go in before either page's own scripts: the window's through `Page.addScriptToEvaluateOnNewDocument` and a reload,
  the Lab page's through a new `smoke({ frameScript })` (dev/smoke.js: a script each new document of an iframe target
  runs first). `requestAnimationFrame` is answered on a 240-a-second grid **only while the page is really being drawn**
  (a real frame came in the last 100 ms), so a hidden window or a parked Lab gets no frames, as on the real screen.
- Every frame callback, timer, interval and idle callback that runs is counted by where it was asked for (file:line),
  per second; the Lab's drawn frames come from its pace counter; endless CSS animations running on visible things are
  listed.
- States, in order, on the bigchat84 profile (40 chats and a 6,000-message one): a chat open after a reply (at 10 s
  and again after the calm start, 75 s), the giant chat opened, scrolled to its top and back, then idle; a long reply
  streaming and the moment after it; the sync panel open; Hearth behind another app; the Lab with a scene playing on
  Auto; the same behind another app; minimized.

### What was checked and found inert (nothing to change)
- `__labHitch` (the hitch recorder) does nothing per frame unless a check started it; `__labPicsSettle` only runs when
  asked (the director's screenshot, capped at 3.5 s). Neither appears in any state's counts; the check asserts it.
- The warm-gap compiles and the upload slices run in the task after a frame only while there is something queued
  (`upArm`, `wgGap`), then stop: no frames asked for with an empty queue.
- The `decOff` worker decodes on demand: no timer, no frame.
- Lazy's idle loading (labload94) waits for the calm start, then loads one group per idle moment and stops: the chat at
  10 s shows nothing of it on a timer or a frame.
- chatwin's idle record reads (cw103) and chat-rest's pieces per frame (cw103) end with their work; a giant chat open
  and idle asks for nothing.
- chatsafe's reader in parts (checksgc104) puts its rows in 10 at a time a frame while a list is being drawn, then stops.
- The sync panel listens to sync's events; the kept-aside view is a dialog with no timer or frame.

## What changed
- **`tools/three-sandbox.html`, `realRAF` (the Lab page's frames):** on a fast screen with the preview capped (Auto ≤ 60,
  30 behind another app, Light mode, a modal over the Lab), after a drawn frame the page asks for frames again only a
  screen frame + 4 ms before the cap's next one, through one timer (`GATE`): one skipped frame and the drawn one,
  instead of three (at 60) or seven (at 30) skipped ones. The cap's own frame-exact rule (`capOk`) still decides which
  frame draws, so the picture's timing is the same; 4 ms of slack covers a late timer (Windows). It never holds frames
  on a 60 Hz screen (nothing to skip: the screen's frame is learnt from the gaps between frames, ≥ 12 ms → off), nor with
  a song or live sound playing (the beat tick's own timing), a sequence, a show (▣ Present), a recording or still, an
  offline render, a frame step, ▶ Play or the export page.
- **`tools/three-sandbox.html`, `mtxTick` (a model's full pictures, modeltex100):** a model kept in the asset cache and
  not drawn was looked at 2.5 times a second for as long as it stayed there; while the Lab is parked or the window
  hidden (nothing draws, so nothing can go up) it now looks every 2 s.
- **`dev/smoke.js`:** `smoke({ frameScript: { match, source } })`, a script each new document of an iframe target runs
  before its own (the Lab preview's counters and fake screen).
- **`dev/checks/fps107.js`** (group `fps107` in `dev/run-checks.js`): the states above with their limits:
  idle states ≤ 1 frame callback and ≤ 3 timers a second, nothing animating behind another app; streaming ≤ 40 frame
  callbacks a second (paced, not the screen's rate); the Lab ≤ 62 drawn and ≤ 140 page frames a second and ≤ 2.5 per
  drawn frame on Auto, ≤ 32 drawn and ≤ 80 page frames behind; minimized: nothing drawn, no frames, ≤ 1.5 timers a
  second; the probes never wake anything. `FPS107_REPORT = 1` reports without failing.
- **`docs/windows-test-plan.md`** §14: Task Manager's GPU % for each state on the real 240 Hz screen.

## What's left
- **Streaming's endless CSS animations** (the busy dot, the stream shimmer, the progress breathing): while Hearth is in
  front and a reply streams they move on every screen frame, so the window is redrawn 240 times a second for those few
  pixels. Round 84 left them on purpose while Hearth is in front (they pause 5 s after another app comes in front). A
  compositor-only animation can't be capped to a rate in CSS; making them stepped (`steps()`) or JS-paced would change
  how they look: a choice for the owner if the PC session's Task Manager reading (state 6) shows a real GPU load.
- **The Lab with a song playing** keeps waking on each screen frame (the beat tick reads the song's clock every frame);
  its draws are capped as before.
- The numbers here are from a faked screen; §14 of the Windows test plan asks for the real one's GPU %.

## For the patch notes
- **The Lab rests between its frames on a fast screen.** On a 240 Hz screen the Lab already drew at most 60 pictures a
  second (30 when you're in another app), but it still woke up 240 times a second to decide not to draw. It now sleeps
  until just before the next picture it will draw: about half as many wake-ups while you watch a scene, and about a
  fifth as many while you're in Spotify or your DAW with the Lab open behind it. The picture looks exactly the same.
- **A model you used earlier no longer keeps the Lab busy in the background.** While the Lab is hidden, a model waiting
  in its memory is looked at every 2 seconds instead of several times a second.
- **Checked again, everything else rests.** An idle chat, a very long chat after you scroll it, the sync panel, Hearth
  behind another app and Hearth minimized ask for no screen frames at all, and nothing keeps moving after a reply ends.
